No Cost EMI explained: what should you check before selecting it?

Updated 4 October 2026 • 6 min read • A2ZBest money guide

No Cost EMI sounds simple: split a purchase into monthly instalments without paying extra interest. In practice, the offer can involve an interest charge from the bank that is offset by a discount from the seller or retailer. There may also be processing fees, taxes on fees, card-specific conditions or a difference between the cash price and EMI price. That is why the safest comparison is always the total amount you will pay.

Important: EMI terms vary by retailer, bank, card, product and date. Always read the checkout terms shown for your specific purchase. A2ZBest does not provide financial advice.

Start with total payable, not monthly EMI

A low monthly number can make an expensive product feel affordable, but the monthly amount alone does not tell you whether the offer is genuinely cheaper. Multiply the EMI by the number of months, then add any processing fee or other charges shown at checkout. Compare that total with the normal purchase price.

How the “no cost” part can work

In many offers, the bank still calculates interest, while an upfront discount from the merchant offsets that interest amount. The result can be close to the normal selling price, but taxes or fees may still apply. The exact mechanism is described in the checkout terms, so do not assume every “No Cost EMI” offer is structured identically.

Four things to check

  • Tenure: 3, 6, 9 or 12 months can change the effective cost and eligibility.
  • Processing fee: some issuers charge a one-time fee even when interest is offset.
  • Card eligibility: the offer may work only on specific banks, cards or minimum order values.
  • Discount trade-off: sometimes a normal card discount cannot be combined with EMI.

When EMI can make sense

EMI can be useful when you already planned to buy the product, the total payable is clear, and spreading the expense helps your monthly cash flow. It is less useful when the EMI option causes you to buy a more expensive model than you actually need.

How A2ZBest will display EMI

On comparison pages, we may show that a product has an EMI option or a representative monthly amount only when the source is current and permitted. We will avoid presenting a monthly number as if it were a permanent price. Retail offers change quickly, so the final checkout page remains the source of truth.

Simple checklist before checkout

  1. Confirm the current selling price.
  2. Check the EMI tenure and monthly amount.
  3. Look for processing fees and taxes.
  4. Check whether another coupon or bank discount is lost.
  5. Compare the total payable with paying upfront.
