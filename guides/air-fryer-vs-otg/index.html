Air Fryer vs OTG: which appliance fits your cooking better?

Updated 4 October 2026 • 8 min read • A2ZBest editorial guide

Air fryers and OTGs overlap enough to confuse buyers, but they are not the same kind of appliance. Both move heat around food, yet they are optimised for different cooking patterns. An air fryer is usually better when you want fast small-batch cooking and crisp surfaces. An OTG generally makes more sense when you want a larger cavity, baking flexibility and tray-based cooking.

Choose an air fryer if speed matters most

A basket-style air fryer concentrates heat in a smaller chamber. That can make it convenient for fries, paneer, nuggets, vegetables, reheating pizza and crisping leftovers. The small chamber also means you can often start cooking quickly. If your weekday use is “make one plate of food fast”, an air fryer is usually the more natural fit.

Choose an OTG if baking is a priority

An OTG gives you a more traditional oven layout with trays and racks. It is usually the stronger choice if you bake cakes, cookies, bread or larger dishes regularly. The cavity can accommodate wider cookware, but the trade-off is more space and usually more preheating.

What about air fryer ovens?

Oven-style air fryers sit between the two categories. They often have a larger cavity than a basket model and may include racks, rotisserie parts or dehydration modes. They can be attractive if you want one appliance to cover several small-oven jobs, but they still do not automatically replace a full OTG for serious baking.

Which one uses less oil?

Both can cook without deep frying. The “air fryer” name is mainly about using high-speed hot air to create crisp surfaces. An OTG can also roast and bake with little oil, but the airflow and chamber design are different. The practical difference is usually texture and speed rather than whether oil is technically required.

Which one is better for a family?

If your family wants quick snacks and reheating, a 5–6L air fryer may be easier to use frequently. If you cook large trays, bake often or want to prepare multiple portions at once, an OTG’s wider cavity can be more useful. For large families that like air-fried food, an oven-style air fryer may reduce the need for repeated basket batches.

Our recommendation framework

  • Buy an air fryer if you value speed, compact size and crisping.
  • Buy an OTG if you value baking, tray space and oven-style flexibility.
  • Consider an air fryer oven if you want a hybrid format and have enough counter space.
