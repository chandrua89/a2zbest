What air fryer size does your family actually need?

Updated 4 October 2026 • 7 min read • A2ZBest editorial guide

Capacity is one of the most confusing parts of buying an air fryer because the number printed on the box does not tell you exactly how much food will cook well in one batch. A 4-litre basket can be enough for one household and frustratingly small for another. The better question is not “how many litres?” but “what do I cook, how much at once, and how much counter space can I give up?”

Quick answer: around 4–4.5L is often practical for one to three people, roughly 5–6.5L gives more flexibility for a family of four, and larger baskets or oven-style models make more sense when you routinely cook for five or more. Treat these as starting points, not fixed rules.

Why litres can be misleading

Air fryers crisp food by moving hot air around its surface. That means a wide basket with room to spread food can outperform a deeper basket with the same stated volume. Fries, paneer cubes and wings generally cook better when they are not piled too densely. If you regularly cook flat foods such as cutlets, fish fillets or toast, usable floor area matters as much as total capacity.

1–2 people: prioritise compactness

For a couple or a single person, a roughly 3.5–4.5L basket is usually the most sensible starting range. It takes less counter space, heats quickly and is easier to wash. Going much larger can still work, but you may be paying for capacity you rarely use.

Family of four: aim for fewer batches

For four people, a 5–6.5L basket usually gives more breathing room. The benefit is not only “more food”; it is the ability to spread ingredients in a thinner layer. If your family eats air-fried snacks together or you want to cook protein plus vegetables in one session, extra basket area can save a second batch.

Five or more people: consider large basket or oven style

Households of five or more should think seriously about 6L+ baskets or 10–12L oven-style designs. Oven-style units often include racks, rotisserie accessories or multiple shelf positions, but they also occupy more counter space and can require more cleaning. They are not automatically better; they are better when you will actually use the extra cavity.

Questions to ask before choosing a size

  • Do you cook snacks only, or complete meals?
  • Will you cook in one batch or are two batches acceptable?
  • Do you often cook whole chicken pieces, fish fillets or wide cutlets?
  • How much permanent counter space is available?
  • Will you move the appliance in and out of a cabinet?

Our practical rule

Buy one size larger than the absolute minimum only if the extra footprint fits your kitchen. Oversizing slightly can improve airflow and reduce batch cooking, but a huge appliance that is inconvenient to store often gets used less. The best size is the one that makes weekday cooking easier, not the one with the largest number on the specification sheet.
